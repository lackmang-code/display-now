<p class="lede">On 23 April 2026, a US company called Lepton Computing sued Samsung Electronics in the Eastern District of Texas. It demanded a sales injunction, claiming the entire Galaxy Z Fold, Flip and Tri-Fold line infringed nine of its patents. Several outlets pointed to the time gap — that the grant date of Lepton's oldest patent is later than the launch of Samsung's first Fold — and cast doubt on the suit's credibility. And yet by priority date rather than grant date, and opening every patent Lepton holds separately, the story splits. Half of them really are late, and half are startlingly early.</p>

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Background and purpose

In patent litigation, "who came first" is settled by priority date, not grant date. Reporting generally cites the grant date alone and reaches the simple conclusion of a "timing problem." CLAIM checks whether that conclusion matches the patent originals, going through every patent Lepton holds in priority order.

### 1.2 Scope

No period was set, because the point of this survey was to confirm when Lepton's patent portfolio actually begins. It covers 2010 by priority date through to the filing of the suit in 2026. The subjects are Lepton Computing (two patents), Samsung Electronics (one), Lenovo Beijing (one), and one litigation record.

### 1.4 Technical classification

Lepton's patents are divided on a single axis, "patent type": the **broad concept patent** on a reconfigurable segment display, and the **hinge structure patent** claiming the physical structure of an actual folding hinge. Lump the two together as "Lepton's patents" without distinguishing them and the timing argument becomes inaccurate.

## 4. Comparative analysis

### 4.1 Lepton's two waves: 2010 and 2020 are different stories

Lay Lepton Computing's patents out by priority date and two distinct waves appear. The first wave is the **"reconfigurable segment display"** concept beginning in August 2010 with a provisional application (US 61/372,391), continuing through several continuations to this day. It covers connecting four or eight 4.3-inch segments with flexible circuits to reconfigure from phone size to tablet size, and claims no particular hinge structure.

The second wave is the **"hinge mechanism having plate stops"** of March 2020. It defines the folding angle range as under 10 degrees fully folded and 170 to 190 degrees fully extended, and claims a structure in which plates extending from the hinge sleeve stop the rotation. This is the patent that can actually be compared with Samsung's physical hinge. And its priority (2020-03-25) is 6 months later even than the Galaxy Z Fold launch (2019-09).

That is, for the claim that "Lepton is the originator" to hold, it first has to be said which wave is meant. For the broad concept it is right. For the hinge itself it is, at least against the Samsung and Lenovo patents confirmed here, late.

### 4.2 Samsung versus Lenovo: the 2014 contest split by 7 months

In February 2014 Samsung Electronics filed a hinge structure making an "accommodation space" where the two bodies fold, supporting the folded display panel so it is not pressed. That patent (US9250733B2) has been cited by 305 subsequent patents, which means it is a foundational patent the industry as a whole references.

In September of the same year Lenovo Beijing produced a different approach: a "double-shaft hinge" (US9611680B2) placing transmission members between two parallel rotation shafts so they turn in mesh. Where Samsung claimed **a space supporting** the folded portion, Lenovo claimed the **meshed transmission structure** itself. Same period, same problem, different mechanism.

A gap of 7 months does not mean "Samsung influenced Lenovo." Given that the two claim structures are fundamentally different, they may have been developed independently. That was not confirmed, though.

### 4.3 The suit: what is it aiming at?

The suit filed on 23 April 2026 (Lepton Computing LLC v. Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. et al, case number 2:26-cv-00338, Eastern District of Texas) has Lepton calling itself "the original developer of the foldable phone," claiming concepts and prototypes going back to 2008. The complaint claims the entire Galaxy Z Fold, Flip and Tri-Fold line infringes nine patents relating to flexible displays, hinge mechanisms and UI transitions, and demands a sales injunction.

On the evidence of the two patents that could be confirmed, this suit is favourably placed in time if it leans on the "concept" patent (2010) and unfavourably if it leans on the "hinge structure" patent (2020). Which of the two each of the nine attached patents is exists only in the court record (PACER, paid) and could not be confirmed in this survey. So this article makes no judgement that "Lepton wins" or "loses."

## 5. Counter-evidence and limits

**The two confirmed patents are not asserted to be the same as the nine attached to the suit.** The earliest by priority (2010) and the closest to a hinge (2020) were chosen from Lepton's Google Patents assignee list; the actual list of asserted patents in the complaint (PACER, paid) could not be confirmed.

**Priority advantage is not read as an advantage in patent validity.** That the 2010 patent is earlier is separate from whether its claims actually cover Samsung's hinge structure. That is for a court to judge.

**The time gap between Samsung and Lenovo is not read as causal.** Filing 7 months earlier is no grounds for saying Samsung influenced Lenovo. The claim structures themselves differ.

**Commentary a tool attaches is not used without verification.** Opening Huawei's CN217847335U, mentioned in a web search result, showed it to be a patent on label packaging material. Entirely unrelated. It was excluded from the article. Cited without checking, a non-existent Huawei patent would have gone in as though it were real grounds.

**A limit in obtaining figures.** The original figures of the Samsung patent (17 figures confirmed to exist) could not be obtained this session because access to the Google Patents viewer was unstable. What was not confirmed is stated as such rather than asserted to be absent.

## 6. Conclusions and implications

The press's one-line summary of a "timing problem" was half right. Lepton's hinge structure patent (2020) is certainly later than Samsung's (2014) and Lenovo's (2014). And yet Lepton's broader concept patent (2010) is earlier than anyone's. The outcome of the suit depends on which patents the complaint actually aims with, and with which claims, and that is not for CLAIM to judge but for a court. What this survey did confirm is that "the whole suit cannot be measured by the single grant date the press cited." A next piece could complete this verification by obtaining, through PACER, the list of nine patents actually attached to the complaint.
