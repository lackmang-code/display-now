<p class="lede">On 15 July 2025, BOE Technology Group and its subsidiary Chengdu BOE Optoelectronics sued Samsung Display in the Eastern District of Texas (2:25-cv-00715). The claim was that the under-panel camera (UPC, the technology planting a camera beneath the screen) structure used in the Galaxy Z Fold5 and Fold6 infringed four of its patents. This suit was one piece of a far larger dispute the two had carried on since December 2022. An ITC (US International Trade Commission) investigation and several district court suits were running at once, and in November 2025 the two companies dropped their worldwide litigation all at once, closing it with a licence agreement worth about 1 trillion won. We open the four patents actually attached to BOE's complaint in the original.</p>

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Background and purpose

The suits between BOE and Samsung Display are not one or two. One ITC investigation and at least 5 district court suits have overlapped since late 2022, and for most of them no specific patent numbers reached the press. CLAIM picked the one suit whose actual patent numbers were disclosed (BOE against Samsung Display, on UPC) and confirms its four attached patents in the original.

### 1.2 Scope

No period was set, because the point was to check the gap between the actual priority dates of the four patents and the timing of the suit's filing and close. It covers 2018 by priority date through to the close of the suit in 2025. The subjects are BOE's four patents (two of them sibling continuations of the same application) and the four parallel suits in the Samsung-BOE dispute including this one.

### 1.4 Technical classification

BOE's four patents are divided on a single axis, "what is changed (the lever)": **pixel structure patents** adjusting pixel arrangement and density, and **wiring structure patents** rearranging the wiring crossing the sensor region. They claim the same UPC goal at different layers.

Those 7 years between priority and filing mean not that the patents are recent but that the dispute caught fire late.

## 4. Comparative analysis

### 4.1 BOE① and ②③: two generations within pixel structure

BOE① (US11037994B2, priority 2018-08-29) is the simplest idea. Place transmitting portions between pixels so light passes through the panel. It leaves the pixel arrangement itself uniform and cuts holes in the gaps.

BOE② and ③ (US12266309B2 / US12307976B2, priority 2018-06-20) go a step further. The screen is divided into two regions: above the camera the pixel density is lowered and transmittance raised, while the rest keeps its density. The claim scope widens toward designing **different pixel layouts by region** rather than uniform gaps. What is interesting is that these two patents are sibling continuations of exactly the same invention. The bibliographic line "Continuation of application No. 17/578,175, now Pat. No. 11,600,230" appears identically on both.

Attaching continuations split from the same invention to a suit as separate patents is not rare in US patent practice. It is a strategy of widening the reach of an infringement claim with slightly different claim scope. From a reader's side, though, it is worth knowing that the number "four patents" is in reality "three different ideas (transmitting portions, pixel density, wiring)."

### 4.2 BOE④: touching the wiring, not the pixels

BOE④ (US11695017B2, priority 2018-10-11) aims at a problem on a different layer. Not the pixels themselves but the wiring crossing between them. Leave the wiring crossing the sensor region as it is and the pixel arrangement becomes irregular and image quality falls. So those wires are grouped and gathered beneath the shading strips. Where the three pixel structure patents solved "how to let light through," this one solves "how to hide the circuitry."

That it was filed by the PCT route (PCT/CN2019/110371), entering the US national phase after an international application, also differs from the other three (all direct US filings or continuations). It may mean BOE had multi-country protection in mind for this idea in particular from the start.

### 4.3 Litigation: why did 7-year-old patents appear now?

All four have 2018 priority dates, and yet the suit was filed only in July 2025. The gap itself is not unusual. US patent suits are commonly filed after the opponent's product has actually reached the market, and after negotiations have broken down at that. It fits the flow of BOE responding with a countersuit only after the Galaxy Z Fold5 (2023-08) and Fold6 had sold in the market for some years, and after the ITC investigation and litigation offensive Samsung Display began in December 2022 had intensified.

The suit indeed closed four months later, on 21 November 2025. Not a win or a loss but "mutual withdrawal following settlement." There is no record of a court judging the substantive infringement of the four patents.

## 5. Counter-evidence and limits

**Whether all four are genuinely the patents attached to the complaint was not confirmed directly from the complaint original.** The patent numbers cited in public reporting such as OLED-Info were compared against Google Patents to confirm they are BOE filings, but the complaint document itself is paid and access-restricted and could not be read.

**The grounds for judging BOE② and ③ "sibling applications" are only the continuation notation in the bibliographic record.** Whether claim 1 of the two is identical down to the wording was not compared. Even as continuations, claim scope can change during prosecution.

**The 7-year priority-to-litigation gap is not read as "BOE belatedly dug up the patents."** Patent suits are generally filed after the opponent's actual infringing product reaches the market, and this suit does not depart from that ordinary pattern.

**That the suit ended in four months is not taken to mean BOE's case was weak.** A suit closed by settlement ends without a court judgement on whose case was stronger. Nor does this article judge which side the (undisclosed) settlement terms favoured.

**Commentary a tool attaches is not used without verification.** A search result summary referred to case 2:25-cv-00430 and 2:25-cv-00715 as though they were the same suit, but comparison against the PacerMonitor original showed the suit with BOE as plaintiff was 2:25-cv-00715 alone. Written without checking, the case number would have gone in wrong.

## 6. Conclusions and implications

BOE's UPC patent suit against Samsung Display looks on the surface like "a head-on collision over four patents," but open the originals and there are substantively only three different ideas (transmitting region layout, graded pixel density, wire gathering), and even those have priority dates all crowded into the single year of 2018. That the suit was filed only in 2025 and ended in settlement four months later carries a strong sense of being a matter of negotiating timing between the two companies rather than of the patents' freshness. Indeed the three other suits between Samsung Display and BOE over the same period were withdrawn together within the same ten days. Read each suit alone and you ask "who won"; place them side by side and they converge on one question, "when did the settlement close." A next piece could confirm from the originals Samsung Display's suit on the other side, whose patent numbers were not disclosed this time (2:25-cv-00412, aimed at the Nubia and RedMagic devices using BOE panels).
