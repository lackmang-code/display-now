Papers published from November 9 to 15 were screened in full across 29 major journals. Of 1,168 papers, 104 were display-related, and 16 of them are summarized below. Electrochromic and reflective displays rose to five papers, the largest group in this period. The two papers covered in depth run separately.

## Modulation range and charge storage in electrochromic materials

In electrochromic materials for smart windows, how much light they block and how much charge they store have pulled against each other. Yuqi Wu and 6 co-authors at Purdue University and other institutions raised both together by introducing a benzothiadiazole acceptor to build a donor-acceptor-donor structure. Intramolecular charge transfer and intervalence charge transfer occur together, narrowing the bandgap and improving both near-infrared coloration and capacitance. The BT-CMP//V2O5 device showed a transmittance change of over 50%, switching times of 1.7 s and 3.8 s, coloration efficiency above 300 cm²/C and an areal capacitance of 38.24 mF/cm². The authors calculated annual energy savings of 155.19 MJ/m² from photothermal regulation, and a flexible prototype kept its performance after 100 bending cycles.

Attempts to combine photochromism, which responds to light on its own, and electrochromism, which is precisely controlled by voltage, in one device go back a long way, but the photogenerated charge and the electrochemical doping equilibrium did not match and stability suffered. Lin Xu and 8 co-authors avoided an electrochemical interface altogether and built an all-solid-state device from a TiO2/CPCN/TiO2 semiconductor heterojunction, stacking a carbon-rich carbon nitride conjugated polymer with TiO2 nanocrystals. Optical modulation exceeded 80% and the coloring response completed within 1 s. The device also demonstrated light-controlled transmittance and high-resolution photochromic images.

Chunxiao Wu and 10 co-authors produced structural color by spray-depositing Ti3C2Tx sheets. Aligning and stacking one layer at a time in a semi-dry state with low surface tension, they obtained colors from yellow to gray at thicknesses between 22 nm and 154 nm. The authors state it is the thinnest skeleton among solution-processed flexible structural colors, that it can be cut arbitrarily, and that its low infrared emissivity suits it to multiband information display.

## V-pits in red InGaN micro-LEDs and AR optics

Red InGaN is the color whose efficiency improves least in micro-LEDs. Hao Wang and 10 co-authors analyzed spatially resolved electroluminescence spectra while varying temperature and current. At low current density, regions with V-pit defects showed a high-energy emission peak separate from the red, and the red intensity in those regions was actually stronger. The authors explain this by emission from quantum wells on the V-pit sidewalls and by charge leakage paths through the defects. They also reported that at an injection current of 1 mA, light output decreases as the temperature is lowered below 160 K.

Diffractive waveguides in augmented reality near-eye displays diffract each color at a different angle, so color does not come out uniformly. Haoran Zhang and 5 co-authors designed a metagrating with non-uniform slant in 4H silicon carbide so that red, green and blue travel at nearly the same angle within a single-layer waveguide. They compared it with vertical and uniformly slanted gratings by rigorous coupled-wave analysis and verified it by finite-difference time-domain simulation. With light incident from above, the target-order efficiency averaged 5.43% over an incidence range of ±45° and the uniformity metric was 46.0%, ahead of the other two designs in full-range uniformity.

Micro-LEDs emit over a wide angle, which makes their light hard to collect in compact optics. Gaoyu Dai and 5 co-authors built and experimentally demonstrated a hybrid scheme in which a Si3N4 metalens at the front forms an intermediate image and a refractive lens with a short back focal length magnifies it. The metalens for 527 nm has a diameter of 600 µm, a focal length of 1 mm and a numerical aperture of 0.3, with a measured focusing efficiency of 40.97%. The transmittance of the assembled system was 43.06%.

## Efficiency and lifetime of emissive devices

The largest figures of this period came from a flexible quantum dot LED. Jing Xie and 7 co-authors used a PET/Ag/IZO substrate and an oxide-free Ga60In40 top electrode to obtain an external quantum efficiency of 30.01% and a T95 lifetime of 20,966 h at 1,000 cd/m². The authors state these are the best values reported for flexible quantum dot LEDs. The liquid metal Ga60In40 absorbs strain, and efficiency barely degraded after bending to a 1 mm radius of curvature.

Guo-Wei Chen and 6 co-authors extended the π-conjugation of a multiple-resonance thermally activated delayed fluorescence emitter on one side only. The design raises the reverse intersystem crossing rate while keeping the locally excited character of the singlet, and it achieved narrow deep-blue emission with a 17 nm FWHM, a reverse intersystem crossing rate constant close to 10⁶ s⁻¹ and a photoluminescence quantum yield close to 1. Suppressing efficiency roll-off in deep blue is the aim of the design.

Siqi Cheng and 11 co-authors made near-infrared emitting devices by adding antimony and neodymium to lead-free perovskite nanocrystals. Lanthanide-doped materials have performed poorly because energy transfers inefficiently from excitons to lanthanides and the 4f-4f radiative transitions are slow. The authors addressed this with a structure in which isolated [YCl6]3- octahedral frameworks confine excitons and local defect states created by antimony assist energy transfer. Yuhan Zhou and 12 co-authors reported tin perovskite near-infrared LEDs whose brightness and stability were raised by handling optical design and thermal management together.

## Charge transport and infrared response in quantum dots

Simon F. Solari and 10 co-authors measured how excitons move in colloidal lead halide perovskite nanocrystal films, resolved in space, time and energy. Nanocrystal films are uneven in size and shape, producing both structural and energetic disorder, and the degree of disorder varied sensitively with the alkylamine ligand used in synthesis. Short alkyl chains increased dispersity, while long ones narrowed the size distribution and also made the particles smaller, making quantum confinement more pronounced.

Two papers came from infrared detection. Xinyu Gu and 6 co-authors aimed to use HgTe colloidal quantum dots in the extended shortwave infrared range between 2.0 µm and 2.6 µm. In this range energy levels are hard to align, leading to poor charge extraction and high dark current; adding PbI2 to the ligand exchange tuned the Fermi level substantially. Xin Wen and 6 co-authors combined AlBr3 ligands with a methylammonium acetate co-ligand on InAs colloidal quantum dots with an exciton peak near 1,200 nm. Under ion-gel gating the field-effect mobility reached up to 0.26 cm²/V·s.

## Photodetection beyond the silicon limit and emissive security devices

For organic photodetectors, low external quantum efficiency above 1,100 nm, where silicon cannot see, has been the obstacle. Kangzhe Liu and 25 co-authors blended the narrow-bandgap donor polymer PDCBT-DP2O with the acceptor BTPCN and obtained an external quantum efficiency of 51.1% at 1,100 nm at −3 V. The result comes from adding dicyanobenzothiadiazole and dimethoxybithiophene units to narrow the bandgap and push absorption beyond the silicon limit.

Seungbeom Park and 9 co-authors at Kyung Hee University, Inha University and Kyungpook National University built a physical unclonable function that uses the optical fingerprint of an emissive material for anti-counterfeiting. Existing optical devices relied on environmentally harmful substances or energy-intensive processes and had fixed emission properties, which left them vulnerable to copying. The authors demonstrated a heterostructure made of mixed-phase multilevel emissive perovskites that produces signals in multiple bands.

Zi Wang and 9 co-authors addressed the coupling between field of view and eyebox in holographic retinal projection. Limited bandwidth made it hard to widen both at once; by combining an electrically switchable dual-period liquid crystal grating with an angular-carrier multiplexed hologram generation method, they obtained a 15° field of view and a horizontally replicated viewpoint width of 25.6 mm. Compared with the baseline configuration, the field of view is about 3 times and the horizontal viewpoint width about 4 times larger.
