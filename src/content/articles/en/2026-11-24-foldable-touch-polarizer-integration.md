## 1. Stack thickness in foldable displays and touch and polarizer integration

Touch and polarizer integration is one of the core technologies of foldable displays. It removes the circular polarizer and touch film that used to be attached separately on top of the panel and moves their functions into layers formed directly on the encapsulation. The polarizing function was replaced by a color filter and black matrix, and the touch function by touch electrodes on the encapsulation.

The reason this technology matters for foldables is thickness. As seen in [part three](/en/article/2026-11-10-foldable-adhesive-low-temperature-modulus), when folding to the same radius of curvature, the thicker the stack, the greater the tensile strain on the outer surface. A 2014 LG Display application (KR102214977B1) gives the thickness of a circular polarizer as 150 µm and states that attaching this polarizer to a flexible panel degrades bending characteristics, and that tensile stress during bending grows as thickness increases (summary). The application text concludes: "tensile stress applied during bending increases as thickness increases."

Touch is the same. A 2019 Samsung Display application (KR102582836B1) divides touch panels into add-on and built-in types and describes the on-cell structure, a built-in type that forms the touch sensor on top of the display panel, as follows: "Meanwhile, among built-in structures, the on-cell structure, which builds the touch sensor into the top of the display panel, is manufactured thin and can therefore facilitate the implementation of rollable display devices, foldable display devices, flexible display devices and the like."

[Part four](/en/article/2026-11-17-foldable-flexible-panel-patents) looked at the circuits and insulating films inside the panel. This part looks above them, at the touch and polarizing functions that moved onto the encapsulation. The patent litigation over metal mesh touch on the encapsulation was covered in the [Issue 8 Y-OCTA article](/en/article/2026-10-06-lgd-tianma-yocta-patent-lawsuit). Before reading the patents, Section 2 first sets out the panel's stack structure and the principles of ambient light reflection and touch sensing.

## 2. Stack structure of foldable OLED panels and the principles of ambient light reflection and touch sensing

Three structures and principles are worth knowing before reading the patents.

### 2.1 The foldable OLED panel stack

A foldable OLED panel is built by stacking many layers. At the bottom is a bendable polyimide substrate. On top of it come, in order, the thin-film transistor (TFT) layer that switches pixels on and off and the organic emissive layer that emits light. Because the organic emissive layer degrades when exposed to moisture and oxygen, it is covered with thin-film encapsulation, which alternates inorganic and organic films.

In the pre-integration structure, a touch sensor film and a circular polarizer were made separately and attached on top of this encapsulation with adhesive. Each film brings its own base film and adhesive layer, so the stack becomes thick. Touch and polarizer integration forms touch electrodes and color filters directly on the encapsulation, eliminating these films and adhesive layers (Figure 1).

### 2.2 Two ways to reduce ambient light reflection: circular polarizers and color filters

Beneath OLED pixels lie metal electrodes that reflect light. In bright surroundings, ambient light reflects off these electrodes, so even when black is displayed the screen looks gray and the contrast ratio drops. The layer that reduces this reflected light is the anti-reflection layer.

A circular polarizer is a film combining a linear polarizer and a λ/4 retarder. Ambient light passing through the linear polarizer becomes linearly polarized, oscillating in only one direction, and becomes circularly polarized after the λ/4 retarder. On reflection from the metal electrode, the handedness of the circular polarization reverses. When this light passes through the λ/4 retarder again, it becomes linearly polarized at 90° to the original and is absorbed by the linear polarizer. However, the light emitted by the organic emissive layer must also pass through the linear polarizer, so part of the emission is absorbed by the polarizer. One application puts that amount at more than half of the emission (Section 6.1).

The color filter approach filters ambient light by wavelength instead of polarization. A red color filter sits over red pixels and a green color filter over green pixels. Light from a red pixel passes through the filter of the same color. Ambient white light entering from outside passes only its red band through the red filter, and the remaining wavelengths are absorbed. The non-emitting areas between pixels are covered by a black matrix that absorbs ambient light (Figure 2). With no polarizer, emission is not absorbed by polarization, and the color filter layer is a few µm thick (Section 3).

### 2.3 Sensing principle of capacitive touch electrodes on the encapsulation

The touch electrodes covered in this part are capacitive. Between drive electrodes (Tx) running in one direction and sense electrodes (Rx) crossing them is a mutual capacitance (Cm) created by the electric field. When a finger approaches, part of the field is drawn toward the finger and Cm decreases. The controller reads at which crossing point Cm decreased and calculates the touch position.

Touch electrodes on the encapsulation are made of metal mesh, thin metal lines woven into a grid. Because metal lines do not transmit light, they are routed only over the non-emitting areas between pixels (Figure 3, right). Directly beneath the touch electrodes, across the encapsulation, lies the cathode of the organic light-emitting devices. The thinner the encapsulation, the larger the parasitic capacitance (Cp) between the touch electrodes and the cathode, and signals generated in the light-emitting device layer mix into the touch signal as noise. This problem, which arose after removing films to reduce thickness, is revisited in Sections 5.3 and 5.4.

## 3. From what thickness to what thickness

The thickness of the polarizing function shrank in four steps. A 2015 LG Display application (KR20230031265A) gives the baseline as "about 150 to 200 µm, the thickness of conventional circular polarizers," and states that a thin linear polarizer without the upper and lower TAC (triacetyl cellulose) films and adhesive layers "can be manufactured at a thickness of 100 µm or less." A 2018 Samsung Display foldable application (KR102604284B1) sets an anti-reflection panel made by liquid crystal coating at 1 to 50 µm and uses 30 to 31 µm in its examples. In the stack of the same company's 2020 application (KR102356652B1), the anti-reflection layer is also 31 µm.

The final step is to eliminate the polarizer itself. The 2014 LG Display application mentioned above placed a color filter in the circular polarizer's position: "The color filter (146) is preferably formed to a thickness of 2 µm to 4 µm, and more preferably to a thickness of 2.5 µm." However, the color filter in this application is not formed directly on the encapsulation; it is formed on a second flexible substrate and attached to the encapsulation with an adhesive layer 5 to 15 µm thick. The thickness of the second substrate is not in the specification, which says only that it can be made of the same material as the first substrate. The reduction is therefore the difference between a circular polarizer of about 150 µm and "color filter 2 to 4 µm + adhesive layer 5 to 15 µm + second substrate."

The touch function went through three steps. A 2015 Dongwoo Fine-Chem application (KR20160085036A) claimed "the thickness of the film touch sensor is 20 to 500 µm," with the base film alone at 20 to 300 µm. The thin touch sensor panel in an example of a 2019 Sumitomo Chemical application (KR102814617B1) is 33 µm (touch sensor pattern 7 µm, adhesive layer 3 µm, cyclic olefin resin film 23 µm), and the pressure-sensitive adhesive layer that attaches this panel adds another 25 µm. Forming touch electrodes directly on the encapsulation removes both the base film and the adhesive layer.

## 4. Types and counts of touch and polarizer integration patents

For this part, Google Patents was searched with twenty-two search queries. Candidates were gathered with fifteen searches of Korean applications; counts by type were then determined with four English searches covering Korean, US, Japanese, European, international and Chinese applications without country restriction, and earliest applications with three searches of the same scope. Counts were taken as one per family. The results total about 6,200 including overlaps, of which about 1,600 applications had their claims and text read. The patents fall into two types: touch film replacement, which replaces the touch film with electrodes formed directly on the encapsulation, and polarizer replacement, which replaces the circular polarizer with a thin polarizing layer or color filters. Three things are identified for each type: the earliest application, the one with the earliest priority date to put the principle in its claims within this survey (Korean, US, Japanese, European, international and Chinese applications); the key patent, which describes the technology in the most detail; and the notable patent, whose product use is unconfirmed but whose principle is new.

An English search combining folding area, touch electrode, encapsulation and foldable returned 291 results: Samsung Display 151, LG Display 64, BOE 25, HiDeep 11 and Tianma 9. Claim 1 was opened for all of these results and for two supplementary searches (folding axis, touch electrode and crack, 49 results; folding area, color filter, encapsulation and polarizer, 173 results). Applications whose claim 1 also includes folding conditions were 14 on touch electrode and wiring patterns in the folding area, 4 on crack, impact and electrostatic protection of the touch layer, and 3 on color filters in the folding area (including applications that appeared only in the Korean application searches). The 14 touch pattern applications break down as Samsung Display 7, Tianma and Visionox 2 each, and LG Display, Innolux and Apple 1 each; 6 of Samsung Display's 7 have 2015 to 2016 priority dates. The 4 touch layer protection applications and 3 folding-area color filter applications are all LG Display's, and the 3 color filter applications were all filed in February 2024. For add-on film touch sensors, among the top 100 by relevance of 2,553 results from an English search combining film touch sensor and thickness, Dongwoo Fine-Chem had 21, Sumitomo 11, Fujifilm 7 and Nitto Denko 5, with materials and film companies accounting for more than half.

## 5. Earliest application and key patent in touch film replacement

Touch film replacement is the technology of stacking electrodes and insulating layers directly on the encapsulation instead of making the touch sensor on a separate film and attaching it. The base film and the adhesive layer that attaches it are both removed.

### 5.1 Earliest application: capacitive touch electrodes on thin-film encapsulation

Within the scope of this survey (Korean, US, Japanese, European, international and Chinese applications, Google Patents, searched 2026-11-15), the earliest application to put touch electrodes on thin-film encapsulation in claim 1 is Apple's July 2011 application (US9400576B2, granted). Of 236 applications with priority dates before November 2012 using "thin film encapsulation," "touch" and "organic light" together, claim 1 was opened and checked for 187 in priority date order. The Korean family is KR101613379B1, whose claim 1 consists of an organic light-emitting diode array, a thin-film encapsulation layer covering it, and "capacitive touch sensor electrodes on the thin-film encapsulation layer."

The US granted claim 1 adds conditions that the electrodes be made as a metal grid and that a black masking layer cover the grid. The black masking layer has openings aligned with the organic light-emitting diodes. The substrate in the examples is polyimide, and the thin-film encapsulation stacks multiple inorganic films such as aluminum oxide and stress-relieving organic films. No thickness figures or folding conditions are claimed.

Other applications from the same period placed touch on a separate panel. A January 2011 Samsung Mobile Display application (KR20120082736A, rejected) stacked a polarizing layer and a touch screen panel in turn on the thin-film encapsulation, and a June 2011 Samsung Display application (US8736162B2) placed a touch panel on one side of a flexible substrate and an encapsulation member. A November 2012 Samsung Display application (KR101453880B1, granted) placed two touch conductive films inside the thin-film encapsulation with one of its inorganic films between them.

### 5.2 Key patent: directly placed input sensing unit and stack thickness ceiling

The application that describes this technology in the most detail under foldable conditions is a 2018 Samsung Display application (KR102604284B1, granted). It came seven years after the earliest application, but it claims a foldable display device and includes layer-by-layer thicknesses and bending test results in its specification. Claim 1 places an "input sensing unit disposed directly" on the display panel, stacks on it an anti-reflection unit consisting of a polarizer and retarder, an upper retarder and a window unit, and then caps the total thickness: "a foldable display device wherein the thickness from the top surface of the input sensing unit to the top surface of the window unit is 130 µm to 540 µm." It is an application that puts touch film replacement and a thin polarizing layer into a single claim.

This application set an upper thickness limit for each layer by testing. For the window panel, "when greater than 130 µm, cracks occurred in the flexibility evaluation, and when less than 30 µm, dent defects occurred in the impact resistance evaluation." The adhesive member above 100 µm and the upper retarder above 80 µm delaminated in the bending test, and for the anti-reflection panel, "when the thickness of the anti-reflection panel (RPP) is greater than 50 µm, delamination occurred in the flexibility evaluation." A dependent claim also claims the condition that the polarizer's polarization axis be parallel or orthogonal to the folding axis.

### 5.3 Direction of development: minimum encapsulation thickness and wiring referenced to the folding axis

Development runs in two directions. One is making the encapsulation thinner. With touch electrodes on the encapsulation, parasitic capacitance to the cathode below degrades touch sensitivity, so LG Display applications set a minimum encapsulation thickness. Three 2018 applications (KR102630171B1 and others, all granted) state, "To this end, as an example, the thickness of the encapsulation (ENCAP) may be designed to be at least 5 µm," and in a 2020 application (KR102937334B1, granted) the value in the same sentence is "at least 1 µm."

The other is touch electrode and wiring patterns in the folding area. With the film gone, the touch electrodes fold directly with the panel. A February 2015 Samsung Display application (KR102283002B1, granted) claimed a minimum angle between the mesh lines of a metal mesh and the folding axis of less than 90 degrees: "Because the mesh lines are formed so as not to be orthogonal to the folding axis, the actual stress on the mesh lines can be reduced." An application filed in June of the same year (KR102411059B1, granted) added connection lines parallel to the folding axis to signal lines crossing it. It solved the problem that "previously, as signal lines crossing the folding axis were repeatedly folded, cracks occurred and parts of the signal lines were disconnected" by letting signals pass through the connection lines even if part of a line breaks. A 2016 application (KR102399297B1, granted) ran the drive wiring diagonally in the folding area.

### 5.4 Notable patent: touch noise from thin encapsulation and touch layer crack prevention

The problems of the film-free structure are also written into applications. Three applications, including a 2020 Samsung Display application (KR102781314B1, granted), state: "However, as display devices become thinner, the thickness (T1) of the thin-film encapsulation layer (TFE) decreases (for example, the thickness (T1) is 10 µm or less), and noise generated by components of the light-emitting device layer (LDL) may affect the input sensing unit (ISU)." In other words, the thinner the encapsulation, the shorter the distance between the light-emitting device layer and the touch electrodes.

A 2019 LG Display application (KR20210085033A, granted) placed a crack prevention pattern formed by patterning the touch panel's insulating layer in the non-display area and filled the gaps between patterns, where the insulating layer had been removed, with an adhesive layer. The same company also filed for an impact prevention pattern on the touch layer (2020) and an antistatic member in the bending area (2023). In this sample, all applications protecting the touch layer were LG Display's.

## 6. Earliest application and key patent in polarizer replacement

Polarizer replacement is the technology of replacing the circular polarizer that blocked ambient light reflection with a thinner polarizing layer, or removing it altogether and reducing reflection with color filters and a black matrix. A 2024 LG Display application (KR20250132818A) summarizes this trend in its background: "In such flexible display devices, structures using relatively thin coated polarizing films instead of thick polarizers have been proposed." It continues: "Accordingly, a CoE (Color filter on Encapsulation layer) structure has been proposed instead of polarizers or coated polarizing films."

### 6.1 Earliest application: black matrix film instead of a circular polarizer

Within the scope of this survey (Korean, US, Japanese, European, international and Chinese applications, Google Patents, searched 2026-11-15), the earliest application to put in claim 1 a structure that removes the circular polarizer and reduces ambient light reflection with a black matrix is Samsung OLED's December 2002 application (KR20040049459A, granted). Of 146 applications with priority dates before March 2007 using "circular polarizer," "color filter or black matrix," "organic" and "external light" together, 95 were opened and checked in priority date order.

Claim 1 is a passive-matrix organic light-emitting device with a black matrix pattern film, consisting of square light-transmitting portions and surrounding light-blocking portions, attached to the outer surface of a transparent substrate. The background describes the light loss of a circular polarizer: "The emission passes through the circular polarizer (17), and at this time the polarizing film of the circular polarizer absorbs 50% or more of the emission." The black matrix pattern blocks ambient light reflected from the metal electrodes. A dependent claim claims a light-transmitting portion side length and spacing of 10 to 100 µm. Thickness is not identified as a problem.

Applications that identify thickness as a problem came later. A March 2007 Samsung SDI application (KR100829753B1, lapsed) states that "film-type polarizers are manufactured by laminating several layers of film, which makes manufacturing complex and costly, and their large thickness has been a problem in realizing thin display devices," and placed a selective light-absorbing layer and a black matrix layer over the non-emitting areas on the device side of the sealing member.

### 6.2 Key patent: color filter instead of a circular polarizer

The application that describes this technology in the most detail in terms of flexible structure and thickness is a 2014 LG Display application (KR102214977B1, granted). A second flexible substrate, on which red, green and blue sub color filters and a black matrix between them are formed, is bonded to the first flexible substrate on which pixels are formed. The top surface of the banks is made uneven so that ambient light is diffusely reflected. The application describes the effect as follows: "In addition, because the circular polarizer, approximately 150 µm thick, can be removed, excellent bending characteristics can be achieved."

The claims cover a flexible organic light-emitting display device rather than folding, but the application identifies the thickness and bending characteristics of the circular polarizer as a problem and replaces it with color filters. Later foldable color filter applications form the color filters directly on the encapsulation rather than on a separate substrate.

### 6.3 Direction of development: coated anti-reflection panels and color filters on the encapsulation

Development runs toward thinner polarizing layers and toward placing color filters directly on the encapsulation. The 2018 Samsung Display application in the previous section limited the coated anti-reflection panel to 50 µm or less. LG Display filed four foldable applications using color filters on the encapsulation in 2022 to 2024. One of them, a 2024 application (KR20250132818A), describes the problem of color filters peeling and rainbow mura appearing in flexible panels, and made holes in the color filters of the folding area: "The foldable display device according to this specification forms holes so that the color filters in the folding area have an inverse taper shape, preventing peeling and cracking of the color filters and overcoat layer and improving panel durability." Another application from the same year (KR20250128708A) put UV absorbers at different molar ratios into two plies of adhesive member in the folding area above the color filters.


### 6.4 Notable patent: polarizer removal and puncture rigidity

The problems of the polarizer-free structure are also written into applications. A 2022 LG Display application (KR20240096016A) states: "Not using a polarizer has the advantage of reducing power consumption, but there is the problem that the puncture rigidity of the foldable display device decreases and ambient light reflectance increases." This application responded by placing a light-blocking portion in a composite functional layer under the cover window, and set the composite functional layer's thickness at 50 to 120 µm "in consideration of folding characteristics."

One application tabulates the relationship between thickness and bending. A 2018 LG Display rollable application (KR20200032914A, withdrawn) mapped polarizer thicknesses of 110 to 210 µm to radii of curvature from 1R to 60R, requiring thinner polarizers for smaller radii of curvature. It is a rollable application, but it shows numerically that polarizer thickness determines the bending radius.

### 7.1 Claims after thickness reduction

The two types followed the same sequence. Earliest applications that removed external components (2002 and 2011) came first, followed by key patents that claimed flexible or foldable conditions and thickness figures (2014 and 2018), and then applications claiming folding-area conditions (2015 to 2024). In thickness terms, the anti-reflection layer thinned from a circular polarizer of about 150 µm to coated types in the 30 µm range and color filters of 2 to 4 µm, and the film touch sensor was replaced by electrodes formed directly on the encapsulation. Each time a film and adhesive layer were removed, the folding stack became thinner.

Subsequent claims dealt with problems arising in the thinned structure. As the encapsulation thinned, touch noise and minimum encapsulation thickness became conditions; as touch electrodes began to fold with the panel, mesh lines and signal lines were laid out with reference to the folding axis. As the polarizer was removed, puncture rigidity, reflectance and peeling of folding-area color filters became problems. Samsung Display was first to secure grants for folding-area touch patterns in 2015 to 2016, while LG Display filed for touch layer protection and foldable color filters in 2019 to 2024.

## 8. Limitations and conclusion

The earliest applications are patent search results; there may be earlier disclosures in applications worded outside the search terms or in papers. Thickness figures differ in measurement scope by application. The 150 to 200 µm of the circular polarizer is for the standalone polarizer, the 30 µm range of the coated anti-reflection panel is for that layer alone, and the color filter application gives only the thicknesses of the color filter layer and adhesive layer, not of the second flexible substrate. No application in this search gave a figure for the total thickness of color filters formed directly on the encapsulation (CoE) and the touch electrode layer. The film touch sensor values are examples from materials companies and may differ from the films actually used by panel makers.

All three foldable color filter applications claiming folding-area conditions are under examination. The key patent for polarizer replacement claims a flexible rather than a foldable display device. Which application was used in which product cannot be confirmed from patents.

Touch and polarizer integration is a technology for reducing the thickness of the foldable stack. A circular polarizer of about 150 µm and a film touch sensor (a 33 µm thin panel plus a 25 µm adhesive layer) were replaced by color filters and directly formed touch electrodes on the encapsulation. In this survey, the earliest applications are US9400576B2 (2011) for touch film replacement and KR20040049459A (2002) for polarizer replacement, and the key patents are KR102604284B1 (2018) and KR102214977B1 (2014). Claims after thickness reduction moved to touch noise from thin encapsulation, breakage of wiring crossing the folding axis and peeling of folding-area color filters.

The next part reads the components below this stack: back-side parts such as the digitizer, heat dissipation sheet, cushion layer and shielding layer. The order of parts may change as the series proceeds.
