## 1. Bright spot defects in foldable panels

The internal structure of the flexible panel, that is, the layout of pixel circuits, insulating films and the encapsulation layer, is one of the core technologies of a foldable display. Each time the panel folds, the folding area takes bending stress, and the panel also takes impact loads from pen tips transmitted through a thin, low-modulus stack. [Part 3](/en/article/2026-11-10-foldable-adhesive-low-temperature-modulus) looked at the adhesive layers that join the layers above and below the screen. This part looks at the panel held between those adhesive layers, and specifically at the circuits and films inside it.

A defect often cited for foldable panels is the bright spot. It is a pixel defect in which a pixel that should display black keeps emitting. A 2022 Samsung Display application (KR20240036773A) put it this way: "In foldable products, wiring, circuits and the like may be damaged by external impact, and bright spots may consequently occur on the display screen."

A foldable display lowers the thickness and modulus of the cover window and the stack beneath it so that they can survive folding. As a result, impact loads from objects with a small contact area, such as a pen tip, reach the thin-film transistor (TFT) layer. Patents measure this impact with pen drop and ball drop tests. A standard pen or a steel ball is dropped from a height of a few cm, and the height at which a bright spot or crack first appears is recorded.

A bright spot appears when a transistor in the pixel circuit opens or shorts so that the emitting element cannot be turned off. The application above cites both cases: "For example, if the switching transistor (T2) is opened by external impact, or the wiring of the emission control transistor (T6) is shorted by external impact, this may appear as a bright spot on the display screen." A 2023 Samsung Display application (KR20240149496A) also pointed to T3, the transistor that compensates the threshold voltage: "If the second transistor (T2, see FIG. 5) or the third transistor (T3) is damaged, the light-emitting element (OLED) emits continuously, and a defect in which a bright spot is visible may occur."

Following this principle, KR20240036773A claimed a dual gate that splits each transistor's gate in two. The switching transistor connects its two gates in parallel so that it still operates if one opens, and the emission control transistor connects them in series so that it still cuts current if one shorts. The claims, however, contain no folding condition, and the dual gate is a pixel circuit technique also used in ordinary panels to reduce leakage current. This part therefore uses the application only as evidence of how bright spots arise and does not count it in any type of foldable structure technology.

There are also ways to stop the impact before it reaches the panel. A 2022 Samsung Display application (KR20240053731A) stacked a low-modulus soft layer (700~900 MPa) and a high-modulus hard layer on the cover window, and placed a hard pattern of 3~6 GPa only in the non-folding area. A 2019 application from the same company (KR20200128260A) stated that the adhesive film beneath the cover must have a high modulus to prevent cover breakage and a low modulus to prevent bright spots in the panel. The trade-off of the adhesive layer seen in Part 3 also appears under impact conditions. This part therefore reads mainly the applications that change the internal structure of the panel.

For this part, Korean applications were searched on Google Patents with fourteen search queries. These included "folding area, inorganic insulation, thin-film transistor, crack" with 87 results, "inorganic insulating layer, organic layer, pixel, island, impact" with 96, "foldable, sub-pixel, external impact, thin-film transistor, crack" with 85, "folding angle, sensor, display panel" with 37, "foldable, pixel, inorganic, impact, bright spot" with 27, and "pen drop, pixel, thin-film transistor, folding" with 13, for about 500 in total including overlaps. Many of these claimed the support plate, impact absorbing layer or adhesive layer beneath the panel, or a digitizer. Reading the opening of the claims, applications that claimed the pixel, circuit, insulating film or encapsulation structure inside the panel were selected, and 20 were read closely, focusing on those with folding conditions in their claims. The touch layer structure is covered in the next part; driving circuits and modules are not covered here.

## 2. Three types of foldable panel structure technology and their key patents

Grouping the applications with folding conditions in their claims by structural principle yields three types: device relocation, which removes devices from places where stress concentrates; crack blocking, which cuts or anchors inorganic films to stop crack propagation; and folding angle sensing, which measures the folded state inside the panel. The first two are structures that withstand impact and folding, and the last is a structure that makes use of folding.

The number of applications by type in this sample is 5 for device relocation (Samsung Display 4, LG Display 1), 11 for crack blocking, and 3 for folding angle sensing (Samsung Display 2, LG Display 1). The 11 crack blocking applications comprise 7 with inorganic layer grooves between pixels (all Samsung Display, 2 granted), 2 with inorganic partition walls and high-modulus impact prevention parts (Samsung Display), and 2 in 1 family of spacers that prevent encapsulation delamination (LG Display, both granted).

Each section first gives how many applications fall in the type and who filed them when, then selects the key patents among them. At least one key patent was chosen per type, on three criteria: whether the claims directly contain a condition specific to foldables (folding line, support plate slit, folding area); whether the patent is granted or followed by later applications on the same principle; and whether other applications of the same type presuppose or reuse the structure. Each section describes the key patent and its direction of development first, and appends applications that are not key but have notable concepts as "notable patents."

## 3. Device relocation technology and its key patents

Device relocation is a design that keeps transistors and pixel circuits away from places where bending stress and impact loads concentrate.

There are few applications of this type. In this sample, KR20230159664A was the only application that claimed both the support plate slit and the pixel circuit layout. Applications extending the same principle are 2 from the search "pixel circuit area, wiring area, non-wiring area" (KR20240017278A, KR20240036772A, both Samsung Display, 2022) and 1 that narrowed the support plate slit width (KR20250039551A). The search "folding, gate driver, non-display area, folding line, non-overlapping" returned 1 application that placed the gate driver so it does not overlap the folding line (LG Display, 2015). No applications claiming the direction of wiring across the folding axis or the transistor channel direction were found among the 13 results for "folding axis, wiring, open circuit" or the 48 for "folding area, thin-film transistor, bending."

### 3.1 Key patent: pixel circuit layout in the support plate slit region

The key patent is Samsung Display KR20230159664A (May 2022), which moves the pixel circuits above the support plate slits onto the ribs. It puts the support plate slit, a condition found only in foldables, directly in the independent claim.

When a pixel circuit sits over a support plate slit, there is no metal layer beneath it to support impact loads. As seen in [Part 2](/article/2026-11-03-foldable-support-plate-patents), the metal support plate under the panel has elongated holes punched in a staggered pattern across the folding band. In an embodiment of a 2020 Samsung Display application (KR102356652B1), the holes are 0.15 mm wide and the bridges between them are 0.10 mm.

This application calls the holes slits and the bridges ribs, and divides the panel's folding area into a slit region and a rib region. The emitting elements over the slits stay where they are, and only the pixel circuits that drive them are moved onto the ribs. The original text empties the slit side as follows: "The second pixel circuit (PC2) may not be disposed in the region corresponding to the slit (50S) of the first display area (DA1)."

The emitting elements over the slits connect to the pixel circuits on the ribs through connection lines. The rib region divides into a circuit area where the pixel circuits gather and margin areas at the slit edges: "The margin area (MA) may be an area that overlaps the rib (50P) of the support layer (50) and in which no pixel circuit is disposed." The pitch of the emitting elements is unchanged; the horizontal pitch of the pixel circuits on the ribs is narrowed so that the circuits for the elements over the slits also fit on the ribs. The embodiment is drawn with a PenTile (RGBG) arrangement, and the claims also include the inorganic insulating layer groove of Section 4.

Development has gone in two directions. One consolidates pixel circuits to widen the wiring area that takes the impact. Samsung Display applications from July and September of the same year (KR20240017278A, KR20240036772A) extended the principle of consolidating pixel circuits in one place to the entire folding and non-folding areas. The other narrows the slit width on the support plate side. A 2023 application (KR20250039551A) gave numerical values for the relationship between slit width and ball drop height, and claimed a manufacturing method that shrinks the slits with heat.

The circuit consolidation applications gathered pixel circuits together and widened the wiring area between them. The original text reads: "By reducing the area of the pixel circuit area (PCA) occupied by the pixel circuits and instead increasing the wiring area (WA), which is the space between the pixel circuit areas (PCA), damage from external impact can be prevented."

The two applications group two pixel circuits into one pixel circuit area and widen the same wiring within the wiring area. The claims set the wiring width in the wiring area at 1.5 times or more that in the pixel circuit area, and the embodiments use 1.5~3 times. The non-wiring area, which has neither circuits nor wiring, is 10 % or more of the pixel circuit area, and 10~25 % in the embodiments. Changing the structure only in the folding area would make line-shaped mura visible at the boundary, so the same structure is used in both the folding and non-folding areas.

In the slit-narrowing application (KR20250039551A), a support plate slit width of 200 µm gives a ball drop height of about 2 cm, 100 µm gives about 6 cm, and as the slit width approaches 0 the height approaches about 8 cm. The application claimed a manufacturing method that covers widely cut slits with a cover layer and applies heat to shrink them: "After forming the slit width at 200 um through a process such as etching, it can be shrunk to 100 um."

The support plate slit must be wide to lower bending stiffness and narrow to support impact loads. KR20250039551A addresses this trade-off by narrowing the slit width in the support plate, and the key patent KR20230159664A by keeping pixel circuits off the slits.

### 3.2 Notable patent: split gate driver (GIP) placement avoiding the folding line

A 2015 LG Display application (KR20170013755A) split the gate driver built on the substrate, the gate-in-panel (GIP), above and below the folding line so that it does not overlap the folding line. The original text reads: "Only clock signal lines or voltage signal lines are placed between the divided gate driver lines, leaving only lines of constant width so that folding stress is dispersed at the folding line."

It also gave the reason: "Since the folding line does not include a crystalline active layer, devices vulnerable to cracks can avoid stress during folding." Crystalline semiconductor layers are vulnerable to bending strain, so no transistors are placed on the folding line and only wiring remains. The same application also described an embodiment with "an inorganic film having a slit on the same axis as the folding line" between the gate line and data line layers. This is an early form of the crack blocking type in Section 4.

As the first application in this sample to put the folding line in its claims, it is conceptually notable. However, no later application in this sample claimed the same layout again, and the attention of subsequent applications moved from driving circuits to pixel-level layout. It is therefore classified as a notable patent rather than a key patent.

## 4. Crack blocking technology and its key patents

Crack blocking is a design that cuts or anchors high-modulus, highly brittle inorganic films so that a crack formed at one point does not propagate to neighboring pixels or layers.

This type has the most applications. Of the 87 results for "folding area, inorganic insulation, thin-film transistor, crack" (LG Display 43, Samsung Display 31, Dongwoo Fine-Chem 13), 12 had openings, trenches or grooves in the inorganic layer in the opening of their claims. Of these, 7 had a structure with grooves in the inorganic layer between pixels filled with organic material; all 7 are Samsung Display applications filed from December 2019 onward, and 2 are granted (KR102749503B1, KR20230033253A). For LG Display, 67 of the 85 results for "foldable, sub-pixel, external impact" were checked, but no application cutting the inorganic film between pixels was found in this sample. The 9 that were filtered out all concerned support plate, impact absorbing layer or adhesive layer structures beneath the panel. Instead, LG Display produced a spacer application that prevents delamination of the encapsulation layer above the emitting layer (among 4 results for "folding area, pixel defining layer, spacer, delamination").

### 4.1 Key patent: pixel island structure divided by inorganic insulating layer grooves

Samsung Display KR102749503B1 (December 2019) cut grooves in the inorganic insulating layer between pixel areas and filled them with an organic layer, dividing the inorganic film into pixel-level islands. It is a granted application.

  </div>

The groove width "may have a value between about 5 um and 10 um." The inorganic insulating layer is a high-modulus, highly brittle film such as silicon oxide or nitride, so if the film is continuous, a crack at one point propagates to neighboring pixels. Dividing the film with grooves stops crack propagation at the grooves where the inorganic film is cut.

The specification calls this structure an island directly: "In one embodiment, the inorganic insulating layer (IL) may be separated by the groove (GV) into island-shaped inorganic patterns in units of pixel areas (PXA)." Signals between islands are carried by connection wiring crossing over the organic layer. The aim is stated as follows: "Embodiments of the present invention can provide a high-resolution foldable display device that is robust against impact by turning pixel groups into islands using grooves and organic layers in units of pixel groups, and connecting the island pixel groups using ductile connection wiring."

Part of the connection wiring also serves as a shielding layer that blocks coupling (crosstalk) between the gate node of the driving thin-film transistor and the data line. The specification states that an inorganic planarization layer raises the pixel circuit integration needed for high-resolution, high-frequency (120 Hz) driving even in the island structure. KR20250008714A from the same family was also granted with the same content.

The direction of development is to cut the inorganic film into smaller units and reinforce the cuts with other materials. KR20230159664A (2022) in Section 3.1 included the same groove structure in a dependent claim and combined it with the slit-avoiding layout. Two Samsung Display applications from 2023 added partition walls and high-modulus structures to the grooves.

The March 2023 application (KR20240144713A) placed inorganic partition walls between pixel circuits and connected neighboring pixel circuits with bridge lines crossing the walls. A wall surrounds one or more pixel circuits, and openings or grooves are cut in the inorganic layer between the wall and the circuit. To this it added a composite layer of alternately stacked organic and inorganic insulating layers.

The pen drop height at which cracks appear is 3 cm with neither groove nor composite layer, 6 cm with the composite layer only, 7 cm with the groove only, and 9 cm with both (Figure 5(b)). ### 4.2 Key patent: inverse spacers distributed in the folding area to prevent encapsulation delamination

Cracks also lead to delamination of the thin films above the emitting layer. A 2020 LG Display application (KR20220090183A) described the problem this way: "Cracks form in each layer making up a flexible electroluminescent display due to bending stress, and as the cracks propagate laterally, a defect occurs in which the layers delaminate from one another." When the encapsulation layer delaminates, moisture penetrates and degrades the emitting elements.

This application placed second spacers with "an inverse-taper shape in which the bottom side is shorter than the top side" between the bank layer and the encapsulation layer. When the encapsulation layer is formed over a spacer with a wider top, it forms an anchor structure that interlocks under the spacer's sides. The second spacers are placed in the folding area, and in one embodiment "the distribution density decreases in the form of a normal distribution with distance from the folding axis." Distributing spacers in the folding area is in the claims, and the patent is granted. In this sample, the only application on the same principle was the same company's KR102877167B1, which claims the same priority date (second spacers in the non-display area of the folding area, granted).

## 5. Folding angle sensing technology and its key patents

Folding angle sensing is a technology that measures the folded state using layers inside the panel. Changing the displayed image according to the folding angle requires measuring the angle. A 2015 Samsung Display application (KR102483468B1) gave the reason: "In the case of a foldable display device, the image signal must be changed to match the bending angle of the display device."

All 37 results for "folding angle, sensor, display panel, foldable, sensing" (Samsung Display 28, LG Display 8, vivo 1) were read. Three applications measure the angle with layers inside the panel and 2 use a Hall sensor on the display circuit board; the rest use the measured angle to calibrate a digitizer or fingerprint sensor or to arrange the screen layout, or were false hits. The key patents are two of the 3 in-panel applications: Samsung Display KR102483468B1 (2015), which forms an acceleration sensor on the touch panel, and LG Display KR20200142394A (2019), which measures the angle from the resistance of panel wiring without a separate sensor. Their measurement principles differ, and both are granted.

### 5.1 Key patent: acceleration sensor on the touch panel

KR102483468B1 (October 2015) formed acceleration sensors on the touch panel of the two folding faces. Each sensor has a motion electrode between two electrodes, and the folding angle is calculated from the coordinate signals of the two sensors. One driver IC processes both touch coordinates and folding angle. It is the first application in this sample to put folding angle measurement in its claims, and it is granted.

### 5.2 Key patent: folding angle measured from panel wiring resistance

KR20200142394A (June 2019) measured the folding angle from changes in the resistance of panel wiring. Wiring resistance is determined by resistivity, length and cross-sectional area. When the panel folds, wiring at the folding boundary is stretched so that its length increases and its cross-sectional area decreases, raising the resistance; when the panel unfolds, the resistance falls. Measurement is made either directly on the panel wiring or on the resistance of the support that holds the panel. The original text states the advantage: "The method of directly measuring wiring resistance can reduce the thickness and cost of a foldable display."

### 6.1 Common direction and trade-offs across the three types

Arranging the key and notable patents by priority date shows the design moving down to the pixel level. The 2015 notable patent placed the gate driver outside the folding line, and the 2019 key patent divided the inorganic insulating layer with grooves at every pixel. The 2022 key patent moved the pixel circuits of display elements over support plate slits onto the ribs. Foldable panel patents have been relocating devices vulnerable to impact and bending stress away from the folding line and support plate slits.

Relocation and division come with trade-offs. Placing more circuits on the ribs requires narrowing the circuit pitch, and consolidating circuits to widen the wiring area shrinks the pixel circuit area. With grooves and partition walls, wiring connecting neighboring pixels must run over the organic layer. The applications wrote these conditions into their claims as circuit layout, wiring width and planarization layer configuration.

Folding angle sensing moved from separate components to layers the panel already has. The 2015 key patent formed a separate acceleration sensor on the touch panel, the 2019 key patent used panel wiring resistance, and the 2024 application used auxiliary electrodes in the touch sensor layer.

## 7. Limitations and conclusion

The 20 applications read here are Korean applications found on Google Patents with a limited set of search queries. Topics for which no claimed application was found, such as wiring direction across the folding axis or transistor channel direction, were excluded. Pixel structure applications came mostly from Samsung Display, and gate driver, inverse spacer and angle sensing wiring applications mostly from LG Display, but this is a result of the sample and should not be read as a difference in company strategy. The key patents were chosen by the three criteria in Section 2 (at least one per type), not by market share or citation count. General techniques such as the dual gate in pixel circuits, which are cited as bright spot countermeasures in foldables but have no folding condition in their claims, were excluded from the types.

More than half of the 20 applications are under examination, and one of the five key patents (KR20230159664A) is also under examination. Applications that are not granted should be read only for their technical content. Pen drop and ball drop heights are values for samples and conditions chosen by the applicants. Which application went into which product cannot be confirmed from patents. Figures 2 and 5 were drawn by DISPLAY NOW from multiple applications, and the rest are original patent drawings. Proportions in patent drawings do not represent actual dimensions.

Outside the three types, there is also an approach that changes the substrate itself. A foldable panel is usually made by coating polyimide (PI) on carrier glass, building the circuits, then separating the glass by laser lift-off (LLO) and leaving the PI as the substrate. A 2024 LG Display application (KR20250130039A) described the limits of this process: "Because part of the PI film substrate is separated by a laser lift-off process using laser equipment, defects may occur during the process due to particles from delamination and roughness on the surface of the PI film substrate." This application uses a glass film of 0.2 mm or less as the substrate instead of PI, and thins only the folding area by forming grooves and holes in a single etch. The aim is for a buffer part to shift the neutral plane of the folding area and reduce stress in the emitting element layer, and for glass, stiffer than PI, to reduce creases and hinge mura from repeated folding. It is still under examination and is the only application in this direction in this sample, so it is noted here rather than set up as a type.

Foldable panel structure technology can be organized into three types: device relocation, crack blocking and folding angle sensing. The key patents are KR20230159664A for device relocation, KR102749503B1 and KR20220090183A for crack blocking, and KR102483468B1 and KR20200142394A for folding angle sensing. The first two types have developed toward relocating devices vulnerable to impact and bending stress away from the folding line and support plate slits, and dividing the remaining inorganic films into pixel-level islands. Folding angle sensing has moved from separate sensors to panel wiring and the touch sensor layer.

The next part reads the integration of the touch sensor and polarizing function placed on the encapsulation layer. The sensor layer used for folding angle sensing in Section 5 is its subject. The order of parts may change as the series proceeds.
