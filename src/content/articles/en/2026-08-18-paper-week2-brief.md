From Nature Photonics to ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, PEER sweeps 12 high-impact interdisciplinary journals each week and picks out only the papers that touch the display industry. The cutoff is the date of first online publication (2026-08-10 to 08-16). This week 16 were caught out of the 865 published in that window.

> **The 2 headline papers of the week were treated as in-depth articles →**
> [Why IGZO, a display backplane material, went to memory](/en/article/2026-08-18-dongguk-ald-igzo-vrram)
> [A light-emitting layer that heals itself in two hours at room temperature](/en/article/2026-08-18-self-healing-wearable-peled)

## The other 14

### 1. Perovskite LEDs have reached 30% efficiency; what is left is lifetime

*Advanced Materials · 08.12 · Ying Liu, Beijing Institute of Technology and others · review*

With the external quantum efficiency of quasi-2D perovskite LEDs now up to around 30%, the diagnosis is that the only bottleneck to commercialization is operational stability. It sorts degradation into intrinsic and extrinsic factors and bundles stabilization strategies together.

### 2. A solution-processed OLED built on a hot exciton channel

*Adv. Functional Materials · 08.11 · Qinyu Zhu (Changzhou University), corresponding Jun Yeob Lee (Sungkyunkwan University) and Lei Hua*

Using benzotri(triazole) as an acceptor, a new hybridized local and charge-transfer (HLCT) molecule was designed. Blue emission at 440 to 490nm in toluene solution, photoluminescence quantum yield 89 to 99%. The focus is on actually opening the "hot exciton" pathway in solution-processed OLEDs. Korean groups (Sungkyunkwan University and Suwon University) took part as corresponding authors.

### 3. 99% color gamut from lead-free tin perovskite

*Adv. Functional Materials · 08.12 · Xinzhen Ji, Zhengzhou University*

Applying reduction and passivation at once with 3,5-difluorophenylhydrazine, they suppressed both the chronic oxidation of tin from the 2+ to the 4+ state and excessively fast crystallization. In pure red emission they reached 99% of the Rec.2020 gamut. It is lead-free material research aimed at active-matrix (AM) displays.

### 4. Perovskite quantum dots made in minutes at room temperature

*Adv. Functional Materials · 08.10 · Anna Abfalterer, LMU Munich and others · OA*

Using zwitterionic ligands, strongly quantum-confined dots were synthesized at room temperature within minutes. Photoluminescence quantum yield up to 86% in blue emission, narrow linewidth, stable for months in air. Wavelength was tuned to cyan by ligand concentration and from ultraviolet to deep red by halide exchange.

### 5. Catching smart windows that glowed on their own under strong light

*Adv. Functional Materials · 08.10 · Zhenyuan Mei, Shenzhen University and Shantou University*

Electrochromic devices had the problem of uneven coloration and unwanted emission under strong illumination. Putting photoresponsive side chains into a conjugated polymer to control ion transport and excited-state energy dissipation together, they improved coloration uniformity and suppressed the emission.

### 6. A radical emitter revived by deuteration

*Nature Communications · 08.10 · Sheng-Fu Wang, National Taiwan University and National Taiwan Ocean University · OA*

Replacing every C-H in a radical emitter with deuterium raised the photoluminescence quantum yield from 24.0% to 30.7%, and the device recorded a maximum external quantum efficiency of 10.81% at 800nm. It is a result for near-infrared OLEDs, but it has value for materials people in pinning down at the level of vibrational modes how deuteration acts on efficiency and lifetime. It is currently an unedited early-access version, so the figures may change on formal publication.

### 7. How to open TADF inside a crystal lattice

*Nature Communications · 08.15 · Xiangchun Li, corresponding Wen-Yong Lai, Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications · OA*

Covalent organic frameworks (COFs) throw away 75% of electrically generated excitons as triplets. The problem was a contradiction: recovering triplets requires the molecule to be flexible, while keeping crystallinity requires it to be rigid. By making nanopores hold donor and acceptor in a particular conformation, the singlet-triplet energy gap was narrowed to 0.014eV, and a solution-processed OLED was demonstrated.

### 8. How liquid crystal polymers twist themselves

*Advanced Science · 08.13 · Laurens T. de Haan, South China Normal University · review · OA*

A review pulling together theory and experiment on how liquid crystal polymer networks deform into helical shapes under stimulus. It covers how anisotropy, alignment mismatch and aspect ratio determine the final shape.

### 9. Kyung Hee University's narrowband red OLED closing on BT.2020

*Small · 08.12 · Meghana Tirupati, corresponding Jang Hyuk Kwon, Kyung Hee University · OA*

Attaching dibenzofuran to suppress vibrational coupling in a multi-resonance framework, they obtained red emission at 613nm with a full width at half maximum of 32nm. In a phosphor-sensitized device using an exciplex host they recorded a maximum external quantum efficiency of 31.63%, roll-off under 1% at 5,000cd/m², and LT90 of 571 hours. In a top-emission structure they narrowed it to a full width at half maximum of 23nm and CIE (0.68, 0.32). The work was carried out under a Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy materials and components technology development project.

### 10. How pyrene liquid crystals stack decides the emission color

*Small · 08.10 · Minwoo Rim, Jeonbuk National University and others*

Making a new pyrene-based liquid crystal mesogen, they traced how the way molecules stack with solvent polarity and concentration governs the final emission properties. It is work connecting nanoscale structure to macroscale optical properties.

### 11. Perovskite quantum dots drawn out like thread

*Laser & Photonics Reviews · 08.11 · Hailong Wang, Qingdao University of Science and Technology and others · review*

A survey of the move to put perovskite quantum dots onto fiber-shaped optoelectronic devices. Being weavable straight into fabric makes it worth reading as a materials trend on the wearable display side.

### 12. Ways to raise the efficiency of all-inorganic perovskite LEDs

*Laser & Photonics Reviews · 08.15 · Yang Yue and Peifen Zhu · review*

It sweeps efficiency-raising strategies for all-inorganic perovskite LEDs such as composition tuning, defect passivation and low-dimensional structuring, and points at the structural reason why efficiency and stability do not advance together.

### 13. A perovskite laser stamped out by nanoimprint

*Science Advances · 08.13 · Xue-Zhou Wang, corresponding Ni Zhao, Chinese University of Hong Kong · OA*

They identified how quasi-2D perovskite deforms with temperature and phase when pressed. The core finding is that stamping in the cubic phase improves viscoelastic deformability and hardness at the same time. Using this they made a perovskite laser with a Q factor of 36,000 and a threshold of 5µJ/cm², holding single-mode lasing for more than 151 hours.

### 14. A white LED made from rare-earth halides

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 08.10 · Songchao Bai, Liaoning University and others*

Doping bismuth into a heterostructure joining 0D and 3D rare-earth metal halides, they obtained self-trapped exciton emission with two peaks at 440nm and 590nm. Photoluminescence quantum yield up to 92.12%, with color temperature continuously tunable across the white light region by doping concentration. Being lead-free is its strength.
