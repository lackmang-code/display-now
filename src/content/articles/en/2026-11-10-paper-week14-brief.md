From November 2 to 8, 2026, every newly published paper in 29 major journals was screened. Of the 1,150 papers scanned, 104 related to displays were selected, and 16 of those are introduced here. Two in-depth articles run separately.

This window was concentrated on quantum dot and perovskite emission. A pure-blue quantum dot device and an active-matrix RGB device came out in the same week. An electrochromics paper also appeared, the first in some time.

## 1. A pure-blue quantum dot LED with suppressed roll-off

*Nature Photonics · 11.04 · Peng Bai and 25 others*

Efficiency roll-off has long held back blue quantum dot devices. Raise the luminance and efficiency drops sharply, leaving them unusable at practical brightness. This paper reports minimal roll-off together with high luminance.

## 2. Inverted RGB quantum-dot-electrolyte LEDs aimed at active matrix

*Nature Communications · 11.02 · Yunfei Ren and 8 others*

Inverted devices incorporating an electrolyte were built in all three colors, red, green and blue, with active-matrix displays as the target. Electrolyte-based devices have low driving voltages, but stability has been the open problem. The authors report efficiency and stability together.

It belongs to the same inverted family as the Seoul National University and Sungkyunkwan University study covered in depth in this issue. The two handle the electron transport layer differently.

## 3. The open problems of high-luminance blue quantum dot LEDs

*Advanced Optical Materials · 11.03 · Seongjae Kang and 4 others*

A review of the problems blue quantum dot devices run into at high luminance. It came out the same week as item 1 and is a good overview of what is blocking progress in practice.

## 4. How excitons cross over in TADF-sensitized fluorescence

*Nano Letters · 11.05 · Tunan Kuang and 5 others*

Hyperfluorescence is a configuration in which a TADF molecule up-converts triplets and hands the energy to a fluorescent emitter to produce light. This paper controls that energy transfer separately at the molecular level and at the nanoscale.

The scheme divides efficiency and color purity between two materials, so where and how much energy leaks determines performance.

## 5. Quasi-2D perovskite LEDs improved with a multifunctional small molecule

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 11.06 · Ying Li and 14 others*

In quasi-2D perovskites, phase distribution governs performance. A single small molecule takes on several functions at once to raise device performance.

## 6. Perovskite luminance raised with a five-component A-site mix

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 11.04 · Xingyu Ye and 11 others*

Five cations were mixed on the A site of the perovskite lattice to tune entropy and enthalpy together, an approach aimed at high luminance and stability.

## 7. Host trap polarity as a lever on exciton behavior

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 11.05 · Yansong Li and 6 others*

The polarity of the traps created by the host material changes how charge is buffered, and that in turn regulates exciton dynamics, the authors report. Triplet energy is not the only criterion for choosing a host.

## 8. A hybrid white OLED with suppressed roll-off

*Optics Express · 11.05 · Chenxi Sun and 8 others*

Efficiency roll-off was suppressed in a hybrid white device that uses fluorescence and phosphorescence together. The configuration targets lighting and backlights.

## 9. A review of quantum dot color conversion layers for displays

*Small · 11.06 · Kiwook Kim and 3 others · DGIST · CC BY*

A quantum dot color conversion layer sits on a blue light source to produce red and green. High emission efficiency and a narrow full width at half maximum make it useful for widening the color gamut. This review covers everything from materials to patterning.

It comes from a Korean research team.

## 10. Indium composition in pyramidal micro-LEDs

*Applied Physics Letters · 11.02 · Ivan Martinovic and 6 others · CC BY*

The growth rate and indium incorporation of five InGaN/GaN quantum well pairs were studied in pyramidal micro-LEDs made by selective area growth. Emission properties were examined with electroluminescence and cathodoluminescence.

In a pyramid structure, each side has a different crystal facet and takes up a different amount of indium, which determines color uniformity.

## 11. State-of-charge monitoring that decouples capacity and electrochromism

*Nano Letters · 11.03 · Huajie Zhang and 12 others*

This structure separates the color change of an electrochromic device from its charge capacity, so that the state of charge can be read from the color in real time. It is closer to smart windows and energy storage than to displays, but its handling of electrochromic materials touches on reflective displays.

## 12. A cholesteric liquid crystal tuned across the full spectrum by visible light

*Advanced Optical Materials · 11.05 · Yu Ma and 2 others*

In a cholesteric liquid crystal, the helical pitch sets the reflected wavelength. Visible-light irradiation changes that pitch, tuning the reflected color across the entire visible range.

## 13. Ultrascaled IGZO transistors aimed at 2T0C DRAM

*ACS Nano · 11.06 · Kexin Wang and 17 others*

This work shrinks oxide semiconductor transistors drastically for use in memory. It targets monolithic 3D integration and addresses reliability as well. IGZO, which started out in display backplanes, keeps moving toward memory.

## 14. Subthreshold swing in amorphous In-Ga-O transistors

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 11.06 · Jae Young Lee and 4 others*

Trap states were locally engineered to adjust the subthreshold swing. Subthreshold swing sets the voltage needed to turn a transistor on and off, and that feeds directly into panel power consumption.

## 15. A layer-based holographic 3D display with extended depth of focus

*Laser & Photonics Reviews · 11.08 · Fan-Chuan Lin and 6 others*

In a method that computes holographic displays layer by layer, depth of focus was extended and image quality improved together. Research on multi-stage holography tolerant of alignment errors also came out the same week.

## 16. A silver nanowire transparent electrode with tunable work function

*ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · 11.02 · Yuanju Zhang and 7 others*

Silver nanowires were combined with MXene (Mo₂CTx) to form a transparent electrode whose work function is tuned chemically. Work function sets the charge injection barrier, so the same electrode can be matched to different devices.
