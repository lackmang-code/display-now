<p class="lede">In 2014 Corning had a patent granted on stacking bendable glass into an assembly. Schott raised the question with the European Patent Office (EPO) of whether that patent was actually reproducible. The board revoked two of them and one remained under examination. Corning retains a right of appeal. This is not conjecture or narrative but the published EPO case record. Taking that litigation as a starting point, we opened all seven foldable glass patents in priority order, from Corning in 2014 to Dowoo Insys in 2024.</p>

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Background and purpose

The cover glass of a foldable display carries the contradiction that "the thinner it is the better it folds, and the thinner it is the more easily it breaks." How that contradiction is actually being resolved is confirmed from the originals of granted and published patents. The purpose is confirmation, not prediction. What the companies actually wrote in their specifications is carried over as it stands.

### 1.2 Scope

No period was set, because confirming when foldable glass as a technology was actually first patented was one of the core tasks of this analysis. It covers seven patents over 10 years, from 2014 to 2024 by priority date. The companies are Corning, Schott (two), Samsung Display (three) and Dowoo Insys.

### 1.4 Technical classification

The collected patents were classified on two axes, "what is changed (the lever)" and "what is aimed at (the goal)." The levers are three: structure (layer and stack arrangement), process (strengthening treatment and property specifications) and material substitution (changing the glass itself for another material). The goals are two: strength (impact resistance) and appearance (visibility of the fold mark).

## 2. Quantitative sketch

Since this is not an exhaustive survey, instead of counting "how many," the seven patents obtained are arranged three ways — by time, by lever and by figures — to check their relative positions.

The Samsung Display① (2019) to Dowoo Insys (2024) span joined by the dashed curve is where the same idea — "treat the folding region differently from the non-folding region" — reappears five years later at a different company, by a different means and for a different goal. The real first wave of foldable glass patents was not in 2026 but already in 2014 and 2015.

Six crowd into the strength row and one into the appearance row. Dowoo Insys (appearance row) and Samsung Display① (strength row) are both in the "structure" column and both share the higher-level idea of "treating the folding region differently from the non-folding region," but their concrete means differ. Samsung Display① varies the depth of chemical strengthening (the second layer) by region, while Dowoo Insys etches away thickness by region. It is closer to a case of the same idea splitting into different tools for different goals.

The compressive stress ranges specified by three patents — Corning (2014), Schott② (2018) and Samsung Display③ (2021) — overlap broadly around 100 to 2000MPa. Which means that across a gap of seven years, and using different levers (stack structure, glass-ceramic, molten salt process), they arrived at the same numerical range. This overlap does not mean the actual strength of the three companies' products is the same. Measurement thickness and test conditions may differ by specification, so no direct comparison is made (see the counter-evidence in Section 5). The Schott①, Samsung Display① and ②, and Dowoo Insys patents do not state CS numerically and are excluded from this chart.

## 4. Comparative analysis

### 4.1 Corning versus Schott: the litigation that became a patent

In January 2014 Corning filed EP3099642 on a "stack assembly" structure piling bendable glass layer on layer. Glass element thickness 25 to 125μm, compressive stress 100 to 2000MPa, bend radius 3 to 20mm. It pinned down in concrete figures the specifications foldable glass must have, and is the earliest foldable-specific patent confirmed in this report. It was granted in March 2018.

Schott met it with an EPO opposition. The board's issue, before whether the patent was valid, was **"does it actually reproduce if you follow this method?"** However precise the claims, the EPO doubted whether following the procedure written in the specification really produces that result. The sister patents in the same family, EP3099643 and EP3099644, were revoked in May 2020 and January 2021. EP3099642 itself had oral proceedings scheduled for 26 April 2021. Corning retains a right of appeal.

Schott was not idle in this period. In October 2015, 20 months after Corning, Schott filed its own patent (Schott①, later granted as US12130278B2). The approach differs. Where Corning claimed "how to stack it" (structure), Schott① took "how long it goes without breaking" (long-term bending durability) as its subject. Storage humidity 40 to 100%, storage period at least 1 day, probability of failure within half a year below 0.1. It defines in figures the condition of not breaking over time rather than in a single bend. This patent is still granted and active.

In summary: Corning claimed "how to fold it" first, and a good deal of it was revoked. Schott claimed a different question at almost the same time, "how long can it be folded," and survived. Judgement on infringement or invalidity is not this article's part. But that the two companies chose different claiming strategies before the same problem, and that the results parted, is what the EPO's public record shows.

### 4.2 From Samsung Display to Dowoo Insys: the same idea over five years, a different means

In October 2019 Samsung Display filed its "glass substrate" patent (claim 12). The core is simple. Make the folding portion thinner than the non-folding portion, coat both with a chemically strengthened layer (the second layer), and give the non-folding side a deeper strengthening. The goal was strength. Since a thin folding portion and a thick non-folding portion sit under different stress conditions, the strengthening process was designed differently by region too.

In March 2024 Dowoo Insys filed a "method of partially etching the folding region so that it is invisible." The higher-level idea is similar: treat the folding and non-folding regions differently. But the concrete means and goal differ. Unlike Samsung Display①, which used the chemical strengthening process (depth control), Dowoo Insys etches the surface away. The goal is not strength but **visibility**. The problem that a steep etching boundary of 10 degrees or more to the horizontal makes the fold mark visible to the naked eye is solved by etching the slope angle down gently to 0.5 to 1 degree or less.

Whether the two patents, five years apart, cite each other was not checked. No claim is made on citation grounds that "Dowoo Insys consulted Samsung Display." What is left is only the factual relation that the same higher-level idea of "treating the folding region differently from the non-folding region" moved from a glass maker (Samsung Display) to a downstream process partner (Dowoo Insys), from chemical strengthening as the means to etching, and from strength as the goal to appearance.

### 4.3 Schott: a different branch three years later

Where Schott① (2015) defined "how long it goes without breaking" in glass as it is, Schott② (2018) changed the material category itself. Glass-ceramic. A gradient structure composed of outer layers containing a crystalline phase and an amorphous central portion. Varying the degree of crystallization by position, with the outer layer crystalline phase at 5 to 80vol% and the centre at 0 to 70vol%, it presents as a formula a bend radius better than pure glass (900×t mm or less). Its lower thickness bound is 0.03mm, the thinnest of the seven here.

Even within one company, two different branches appeared three years apart: "keep the glass and specify the properties precisely" (2015) and "change the material category itself" (2018).

### 4.4 Samsung Display: one company, three levers

Samsung Display's three patents (① 2019 graded thickness chemical strengthening, ② 2020 Beilby layer structure, ③ 2021 molten salt process) split as structure, structure and process. ② designs impact resistance by placing the Beilby layer only on the flat surface of the folding portion and not on the curved part, verifying it with ball drop tests. ③ precisely controls not the glass composition but the strengthening process (the molten salt's composition and solidification point), preventing solidified residual salt on the glass surface from causing interfacial stress. Which means one company is running levers in parallel rather than fixing on one before the same goal (strength).

## 5. Counter-evidence and limits

**The EPO's revocation decision is not extended into an assessment of Corning's technology as a whole.** What was revoked was a procedural issue over whether particular claims of the EP3099642 family are "reproducible as specified." It is not read as meaning Corning has fallen behind across the foldable glass business. The appeal outcome and Corning's subsequent patent strategy are outside this survey's scope.

**Overlapping figures are not read as a ranking of strength.** The three patents whose CS ranges overlap (Corning, Schott②, Samsung Display③) may each have different measurement thicknesses and test methods, so the overlap is not interpreted as "equivalent strength."

**Citation relations are not read as causal without checking.** That Samsung Display① (2019) and Dowoo Insys (2024) use a similar idea (treating the folding region differently from the non-folding region) was confirmed, but whether an actual citation or reference relation exists between the two patents was not.

**Empty cells in the matrix are not read as "that company is not doing it."** This sample is only seven patents. It does not mean Corning and Samsung Display have no patents using the material substitution lever, only that none are in the sample obtained here.

**Commentary a tool attaches is not used without verification.** In an earlier survey an unrelated patent was hastily linked as "foundational technology of foldables." In this session too, the patent number "Samsung Display① KR102244984B1" was initially used as it stood, and while re-searching for its figures, comparing the applicant directly against the original (the KIPO gazette) showed it was in fact a patent of an affiliate of China's Kunshan Visionox. Passed over without verification, that error would have gone in as it was. It was replaced with the genuine Samsung Display patent (KR102810405B1) and the whole card rewritten.

**A limit in obtaining figures.** Original figures were included for Corning, Samsung Display①, Dowoo Insys and Schott②. Three — Schott①, Samsung Display② and ③ — could not be obtained this session. What was not confirmed is stated as such rather than asserted to be absent.

## 6. Conclusions and implications

What runs through foldable glass patents from 2014 to now is the absence of a standard solution. Corning went first with structure (stack assembly) and took a procedural blow at the EPO; Schott survived with a different claiming strategy (a long-term bending durability specification) at almost the same moment, then branched again three years later into an altogether different material (glass-ceramic). Samsung Display patented three levers at once (graded thickness chemical strengthening, the Beilby layer, molten salt), and one of them (distinguishing folding from non-folding portions) was reused five years later by Dowoo Insys with both means and goal changed, etching instead of chemical strengthening, appearance instead of strength. Which side gained an advantage in actual production cannot be judged from this sample. That is a matter of shipment volume and yield, not patents. A next piece could track the appeal outcome of EP3099642, or look for further cases like Schott① and ② where levers split within one company.
