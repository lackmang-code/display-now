Papers published from November 16 to 22 were screened in full across 29 major journals. Of 1,126 papers, 100 display-related ones were selected, and 17 of them are summarized below. The two covered in depth run separately. The defining feature of this period is that seven holography and light field papers arrived at once.

## Holography and devices that redraw light

Using neural networks in holographic displays speeds up computation, but image quality collapses outside the trained distance range. The response so far has been to train separately for each distance. Yanan Zhang and six co-authors addressed this by building the structure of light propagation into the network design. A single encoder without fine-tuning maintained a distance-averaged RGB peak signal-to-noise ratio of 31.9 dB and a structural similarity of 0.91 over the 2 mm to 200 mm range, and reconstruction still worked out to 500 mm. Numerical calculations and optical experiments on full-color 3D scenes were reported together.

On the light field display side came a Korean study. Wonseok Son and four co-authors used a holographic optical element to project light obliquely, widening view density and viewing angle together. Light field displays have been bound by a trade-off: packing views densely narrows the viewing angle, and widening the angle spreads the views thin.

Peter Ropač's work uses liquid crystals to draw and erase optical elements. A structure rewritable by light alone was used to implement waveguides, lenses, beam splitters, diffraction gratings and all-optical logic gates on a single material. These can be linked into circuits and redrawn within about 100 ms whenever needed.

## Emissive materials and devices

Aron J. J. Dahlberg and 18 co-authors arranged nanoparticles regularly on top of an OLED to control the direction and spectrum of the outgoing light. They reported that designing the array geometry together with the device structure can raise the spectral and angular characteristics of electroluminescence by up to 30%.

In 2D perovskites, excitons are tightly bound, so emission is bright but charge separation is difficult. Jiakai Yan and 14 co-authors engineered the band edges of the organic and inorganic layers in (AE3T)PbI₄ to hybridize the frontier states. They created dipolar excitons that are spatially indirect yet retain substantial optical transition strength, overcoming the trade-off between the two.

Koshi Oi and six co-authors addressed the voltage limit in electrolyte-gated monolayer light-emitting devices. The electrochemical stability window of the electrolyte was the obstacle. They formed a lateral PN junction in monolayer WSe₂ by electric double layer doping, then froze it at 10 K under bias to fix the ion configuration. The accumulated charge remained, allowing up to 20 V, and current density reached 0.8 MA/cm². Since freezing is a prerequisite, this is for now a condition for observing physics.

In white OLEDs, Yuwen Chen and eight co-authors made PI2FDPO, a blue molecule with a hybridized local and charge-transfer excited state. An undoped blue device alone reached a maximum forward external quantum efficiency of 8.17%, and placing a doped yellow layer near the undoped blue layer produced two-color complementary white. The white device reached a maximum of 25.29% and 80.62 lm/W, and 22.19% and 49.64 lm/W at 1,000 cd/m².

## Quantum dots and nanocrystals

Yun Hai and 15 co-authors re-examined how InSb quantum dots form at the level of intermediates. The size of the zerovalent indium and antimony nanoparticles generated as precursors are reduced determines intermetallic diffusion and growth rate. By switching the precursor halogen among chlorine, bromine and iodine, and tuning the reductant ratio, the indium-to-antimony ratio from 2 to 1 up to 5 to 1, and the reaction time from 15 minutes to 180 minutes, they obtained monodisperse quantum dots with absorption wavelengths set between 1,100 nm and 2,200 nm. Temperature-varied experiments showed that antimony nanoparticles 3 nm to 4 nm in size act as templates for nucleation.

Giovanni Morello and 13 co-authors embedded presynthesized CsPbBr₃ nanocrystals inside electrospun PMMA fibers. Placing nanocrystals in an optically inert polymer raises resistance to moisture and oxidation and allows flexible devices to be made over large areas. Andrey Iodchik and 12 co-authors made copper-iron-selenide nanocrystals by cation exchange, so that with composition the system runs from plasmonic Cu₁.₆Se to one with an iron-induced intermediate band.

For color conversion films, Amal C. Chekkallur and 20 co-authors self-cross-linked CsPbBr₃ quantum dots with light to form a continuous film in a single casting. Even at a quantum dot loading of 60%, sub-bandgap transmittance exceeded 97% and photoluminescence quantum yield exceeded 95%. The trade-off in which higher loading costs transparency was handled with a single process step.

## Pixel density and panel structure

The largest pixel density figure in this period came from the flexible quantum dot LED of Runzhen Sun and 16 co-authors. Electrodes made with a bioinspired molecular-scale tape interface evened out current and emission in microscale regions, reaching 25,400 ppi with an external quantum efficiency of 23.4%. The authors described this efficiency as a record. Performance was maintained after 3,000 bending cycles.

In reflective displays, Xinyuan Ma and seven co-authors addressed the storage stability of an oblique helicoidal cholesteric liquid crystal system. After 500 days at room temperature, the red, green and blue reflection spectra were measured again and showed no change. Reflective displays emit no light and so have a power advantage, but whether the materials last has been the gate.

Myunghoo Lee and nine co-authors examined how far single-layer polychromatic metalenses for virtual reality visors can go in principle. The study points out the intrinsic limits of designs that handle red, green and blue together in one layer. Those who see metalenses as a way to thin optical systems should read it first.

## Photodetection and sensors

Manoj Kumar Rajbhar and four co-authors made an optoelectronic synaptic transistor from IGZO. The on/off current ratio was about 1.06 × 10⁸, the subthreshold swing 110.58 mV/dec and the field-effect mobility 1.52 cm²/V·s. Stimulated with 405 nm light, it reproduced intensity- and time-dependent synaptic plasticity, paired-pulse facilitation, the transition from short-term to long-term memory, and learning, forgetting and relearning behavior.

The organic photodetector of Zhuangmiao Wang and six co-authors switches roles by reversing voltage polarity. In one direction it faithfully detects visible light; in the other it raises gain to sense near-infrared. Linear dynamic range in photodiode mode exceeds 100 dB, and in photomultiplication mode the external quantum efficiency reached 180% at 810 nm. A 4 × 4 array demonstrated mode-dependent contrast improvement under varying illumination conditions and turbid media.

Yinzhu Chen and eight co-authors handled the trade-off between polarization ratio and sensitivity in on-chip photodetectors by decoupling the optical and electrical paths. At 1,550 nm, the polarization ratio reached about 1,000 and polarization sensitivity rose by two orders of magnitude.
